Qualitative Observations

What the Skill Adds (vs baseline)

  • Structured analytical framework: Autonomy/Involvement taxonomy gives users a vocabulary for thinking about AI workflows — the baseline just lists ideas
  • Downstream compatibility: Workflow Candidate Summary with structured metadata feeds directly into the Deconstruct step — the baseline has no concept of downstream framework steps
  • Dual-lens design: Individual vs organizational framing changes what questions get asked and how opportunities are scoped — the baseline doesn't distinguish
  • Process discipline: Memory scan → lens → interview → report → candidates is a structured progression. The baseline jumps straight to recommendations
  • Organizational metadata: Business Objective, Stakeholders, and Success Metrics on each card force strategic alignment — the baseline mentions these implicitly but doesn't structure them

What the Baseline Does Better

  • More actionable/tactical: The baseline suggests specific tools (Zapier, Looker Studio, Otter.ai) and gives implementation steps. The skill focuses on classification over implementation.
  • Time/effort estimates: The baseline includes "Time saved: 2-3 hours/week" and effort estimates. The skill report doesn't quantify impact.
  • Quick wins: Eval 3 baseline includes "Quick Wins (This Week)" section with 3 concrete exercises. The skill's "Getting started" is per-card rather than a prioritized action list.
