# The review

Read at the start of Part 4, before writing the review, and whenever the review is shown again. The consequential points, the approval question and the close are in SKILL.md (*Phase 4*). Section names in italics are SKILL.md's unless this file has them.

**The review** opens with "Here's the plan in brief. The full plan is in [location]." (no files: "Here's the plan in brief. Say 'show me the full plan' to see all of it; you'll get the approved version to save at the end.") and covers, in plain words, with no requirement or task IDs:

1. **How it will run, and how much it decides on its own:** where it runs and how the leader starts it; with or without them; for `Path: none` with planned steps, the recommendation in `variants.md`; then the level, named once, said exactly, its lead-in included:
   - "How much it decides on its own: Deterministic. You give it instructions: it carries out each step you set, and what it produces never changes what happens next."
   - "How much it decides on its own: Guided. You give it bounded decisions with your method: it uses your criteria to decide some things along the way, such as where an item goes or whether work is good enough."
   - "How much it decides on its own: Autonomous. You give it a goal: it plans its own steps and keeps going until the goal is met."
   - Where the leader fixed steps, end it "…, keeping the steps you fixed exactly as they are.": only when a step line in the requirements is marked `fixed (regulation)`, `fixed (policy)` or `fixed (your judgment)`; steps the leader described but marked `flexible`, and steps you planned yourself, are not fixed, so a plan with none gets no ending. The ending takes the place of the line's closing full stop, so the line runs on into it with a comma and has one full stop, the ending's.

   Before writing the review of a `Path: none` plan with planned steps, a `domains` plan, or regulated work planned as Autonomous, read `variants.md`, *The review*: the lines that go before and after the level there, each said exactly.
2. **The skills it reuses, builds or adds to,** one line each: what it does and what it never does. Say "skill" and "agent" only with their plain meaning ("you start it and it follows your steps"; "it works out its own steps and can run without you"), anywhere in the review, and only where that meaning is true of this plan. Where it isn't, use the form that is, said exactly: an agent that runs with the leader, "it works out [the part it decides], and you start it and read what it gives back"; an agent that follows the steps you planned, "it follows the steps in this plan and works out [the part it decides] on its own"; a skill whose steps you planned (`Path: none`), "you start it and it follows the steps in this plan". Never "It runs with you" next to "can run without you".
3. **What it can read and change,** one line per connection, naming any change that can't be undone and where it asks first. A limit from the requirements is said as the rule the workflow keeps, never as a fact about who can open a place you haven't seen.
4. **How each part will be checked,** from the tasks' *Done when*, a requirement said with its own verb and never turned into a Never word ("the brief covers every competitor the question applies to", never "coverage of every competitor"), including any practice inputs you added for a risk; where it helps, a one-glance view of which part delivers what.
5. **Keeping it working,** in one line: each run flags anything it wasn't designed for (plus the re-check and time-saved lines where they apply); for a scheduled workflow, that Build prepares the schedule but never switches it on, and the leader runs it once by hand before its schedule goes on, and again after any change to it while it's live.
6. **The plan check,** said exactly: "I checked the plan against your requirements: [nothing needed fixing. | I found and fixed [N] things; they're listed in the plan.]" With one fix: "I checked the plan against your requirements: I found and fixed 1 thing; it's listed in the plan."
7. **Anything assumed:** every assumption and default from over the cap, each easy to change.
8. **Open items,** and what each holds up, each said as what's still unknown, never as a question: the review's one question is its ask, and an open item is asked when the leader comes back for it (state 7). Write each as one statement, from the plan's own item (model wording: "[Whether, which or who: the unknown] is still open, so [the parts that wait, in plain words] wait[s] for it."), with no label or heading of its own: the review has no question mark anywhere before its closing ask.

It ends: **Ask:** "What would you change or clarify?"

**Changes.** A correction updates the plan, runs the self-check and the plan check, and shows the review again; the plan stays `approved: false`. Lowering the level is always allowed; raising it only when the evidence and the limits support it, otherwise give the reason in one line (D7 when a limit is involved); the same holds for a reply to B54. A change that creates a Part 3 question asks it before the review is shown again (raising the level can create D5 or D4′; removing a checkpoint or moving to running without the leader, D5 or D5″; any change, D7 or N-14). A question a change in the review creates isn't counted against the cap; safety questions are always asked. A raise the evidence doesn't support, insisted on: keep the plan, record the wish in the Approach as "the leader's recorded wish", say in one line it can be revisited after real runs with "change my workflow. My files are in [location].", and move on to approval.
