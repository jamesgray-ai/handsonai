# The light interview

Read as soon as the track is judged light, before the N-L1 message, since item 0 is in it. It runs in place of Parts 1 to 5. The light track plays back what the backlog card holds (item 0), asks how runs start where needed, asks three safety questions, captures five things, and fills defaults for everything else, shown once to correct. The files, formats, the rules for every message, the confidential check, the progress file, the preview, the close and the registry write are the same as on the full track. Every quoted line is said exactly; each question is its own message.

There are no "Part N of 5" signposts on this track, and N-D17 is never said on it. The questions it borrows keep their full-track wording, *Why* and the rest, from the part files SKILL.md has you read for this track. The policy question is never asked on this track.

## The N-L1 message and item 0

One message, in this order: N-L1, then the light line with its count (SKILL.md, *The track*, word for word), then item 0. It stays within 170 words.

**0. Item 0.**
- **A backlog pick, or running work Analyze recorded:** N-D23, word for word (`part-2-scope-and-a-name.md`, *The card playback*). For running work, the `Runs today` row is played back in the same message.
- **Cold entry, and other running work:** a name and a description in one message. Run Part 2's boundary tests yourself first (when they find several workflows, Part 2's breakdown runs). Then one message (model wording, one question) plays back the description in Part 2's shape, which carries the trigger and the deliverable, with the name you recommend and one or two others in Part 2's style, ending "What would you change or clarify?" (for illustration only; use the leader's own work: "I'd call it Newsletter Draft (or Weekly Newsletter, or Newsletter Writing), described as: after you've gathered the week's notes, drafts the newsletter, ready for you to edit and send. What would you change or clarify?"). For running work brought in, play back the `Runs today` row in the same message.
- **In personal life,** Part 2's two personal questions ("What starts it?", "What do you end up with?") come first, each in its own message, before item 0's message.

The reply confirms the trigger, the deliverable, the name and the description together. The progress file is first saved after it, silently, then after each item (`Next: Light — [the safety questions | the goal | what it must get right | what it works from | the rules | the practice runs | the defaults]`).

## The order after item 0

Each in its own message, in this order, skipping any that doesn't apply. A redraft statement, or N-D6 (the law question's regulated line), when one is due, always opens the message it's in.

1. **A "Not clear yet" question** (`part-2-scope-and-a-name.md`, *"Not clear yet"*): the trigger, then the deliverable; its redraft statement ("So the description now reads: [description].") opens the next message.
2. **How runs start, then how soon,** where asked (`part-2-scope-and-a-name.md`, *How runs start*): asked under the same conditions as on the full track; stated, not asked, when the trigger is something the leader does. A schedule alone doesn't make the workflow full: the light criterion stays "the leader takes part in each run".
3. **The first safety question's message:** "About [M] questions left." (only when 1 or 2 was asked, so it never follows the light line's count directly) + the folder line, "Everything for this workflow goes in its own folder." (left out when the progress file's `Said:` line or notes show it was said) (+ the process line, with a registry and no backlog node, or the no-process question in its own message first, `part-2-scope-and-a-name.md`) + live writes, the first safety question.
4. **Taking part,** stated or asked.
5. **The law question.**
6. **Item 1, what it's for,** its message opening with "About [M] questions left.".

## The three safety questions

Right after item 0 and any "Not clear yet" and start questions, before the five things, so a switch to full lands before the light items are spent. In this order, each as the full track words it, with its *Why*:
1. **What it sends or changes:** Part 5's live-writes question, in its no-tags form (no steps or rules exist yet). A draft saved only where the leader alone sees it, which they can delete, isn't a sign of risk; sending, posting, or changing a record others see is. A write's place, if the reply gives one, goes in the defaults message's Security row; there's no question about it.
2. **With or without the leader:** Part 5's taking-part question, stated when item 0's reply or a checkpoint the leader performs already settles it, with its conflict line. Asked this early, it's usually a question.
3. **A law or regulation:** Part 2's question, with "Which one? A name or a description is fine." and its regulated line on a yes. Skipped in personal life (`Governing regime: None`). A yes is a sign of risk: "Which one?" is still asked first, in its own message; then N-D6 opens the switch message, ahead of N-L2. A "not sure" goes straight to the switch message.

Any answer that shows a sign of risk (a change that can't be undone, a send, a change others see, running without the leader, a regulation, a "not sure" to the law question, confidential or outside content): N-L2, and the full track carries on (*Switching to full*).

## The five things

**The count line,** "About [M] questions left.", opens the message of the first question of each of items 1 to 5 (after only a redraft statement, when one is due), with that item's figure (SKILL.md, *How much is left*). Never twice in a row.

**A sign of risk at any item.** Whenever a Context Inventory row is saved as `Confidential` or `Regulated`, or a source is `External`, or the leader says something they pasted (a document at N-D1, a real example, an input) holds client, staff or personal details, that's a sign of risk: N-L2, and the full track carries on. A row saved `Confidential` or `External` only as the safe fallback for something the leader hasn't said (SKILL.md rules 7 and 13) is no sign of risk (SKILL.md, *Light needs every one of these*). The confidential check is still said before that content is saved.

**1. The goal.** The privacy line (`part-3-the-outcome.md`), if it hasn't been said; Part 3's real-example question (on a yes, the example is pasted, read and saved as Part 3 says; what the leader started from is asked with the practice runs, item 5); the recipe line; "say it", with "Once it's ready, who gets it?" where needed; the blank questions only where nothing fills them; and the goal playback with its question and its clause (Part 3's light form). It's the goal's one confirmation on this track: the full goal isn't played back a second time.

**2. What it must get right.** "Make it checkable", then the list played back ("Here's what every [deliverable] must get right:"), each vague word already replaced inside it by a concrete version, tagged *(my suggestion)*, or *(you said)* where the leader gave one, ending "What would you change or add?". There's no separate vague-words message. Then the send-back test with its must-have line. The list is normally three to five checks; nothing the leader named is dropped.

**3. What it works from.** The typical-case question (Part 4's question 6, its first message only), then one draft of the inputs and sources (the requirements template, draft 2's sources and inputs, with the context sentence the first time), ending "What would you change or add?". A source that trips a privacy test (a `Confidential` or `Regulated` row, an `External` source) is a sign of risk: N-L2, and the full track carries on. Then **carry-over**, in its own message, only when `Starts` is a schedule or an event checked on a schedule, or `Per run` is a batch: Part 4's carry-over question, word for word (only [cases] and [deliverable] filled; "already handled" stays), with its *Why* and *Not sure?* (`part-4-the-path.md`). Otherwise record `Remembers: nothing` and ask nothing.

**4. The rules.** The always/never question (Part 4's "Is there anything it must always do, or never do, however it goes about the work?"), then the review question in its no-steps form ("Is there a point where you review or approve the work before it goes further?"), then one draft of the rules and checkpoints in the "When… it…" shape, with standing rules offered, ending "What would you change or add?". Then the standing-rules question, only from the leader's second workflow and when it applies (the requirements template, *Standing rules*).

**5. The practice runs.** Draft 4 with two or three in total, opened with Part 5's opening, word for word: "Here are the practice runs we'll test it on." ("we'll", never "I'd" or "I'll"). Nothing else from Part 5's list: the typical case, real (with a real example, the input behind it is the typical run, and holds the Golden Example in its row); and a tricky one, proposed when the leader gave none. No carry-over, out-of-bounds or rule-aimed input is proposed on this track: Test writes those. Each playback says what should happen with that input, and each input saved gets its `expect:` line from it (the requirements template, *inputs/*). End it with N-D11's light form, word for word: "Change, replace or drop any; keep two or three, including your typical [case]. When we test it, each of these is run and checked against your requirements." Then the real inputs, pasted now. With a real example (pasted at item 1), the typical run's input is asked for only this way, never with Part 5's paste request: in the message after the leader's reply to the practice runs, one line (model wording) names the example, then:

**Ask:** "What did you start from for that one?"

Its answer is the typical run's input. Any other real input named but not pasted takes Part 5's paste request. If, after the leader's changes, fewer than two remain or the typical case was dropped, propose a replacement and have it kept before going on; no tricky case is required on this track. No reference is asked for per row: the Golden Example, when there is one, is the only reference. A password or key in that answer, or in any input pasted here, is replaced, where it stood, with a placeholder ("[password removed]", "[key removed]") before anything is saved, never just left out, and N-F14 is said (SKILL.md). When a kept run is a proposed one (the tricky one, or a replacement), its input is shown before saving, after the real inputs, as Part 5's *The inputs you wrote, shown before saving* says: in one message of its own, N-D25 said word for word in two parts around the inputs: its first sentence, "Here are the practice inputs I wrote.", then each input you wrote under a plain label naming its run, then its second sentence, "Does each look like what you'd really get?", last, so the message ends on the question; the leader's corrections are what's saved. Then the confidential check where it applies, and the inputs numbered and saved.

## The defaults

After the five things, one message (model wording) lists in plain words everything you filled in, never by the file's labels (Business Objective, Baseline, Sensitivity), ending "What would you change or add?". Each row below is said, the target included (with none, say there's no target yet). A source whose details aren't known is said as not known yet, and that alone never turns the track full:

| What | Default |
|---|---|
| How the work gets done | The AI works it out each time, from the goal, the list and the rules (`Method preference: AI works it out each time`; `Path: none — the AI plans each run from the goal`) |
| Decisions | None recorded |
| When it's unsure | "When it can't confidently handle a case, it does its best and flags it for you." |
| Business Objective, Desired Outcome, Measure | From the backlog card, the document or the description; otherwise "—". The Business Objective reads as on the full track: the card's Business Objective on the Organizational lens when it names an objective, otherwise its Goal; neither "protects …" fallback is a named objective (`choosing-a-workflow.md`, *Prefilled*) |
| Baseline | `— · Unknown` |
| Target | "—" |
| Owner, Lens, Stakeholders | The leader; Individual; none, unless the card says otherwise |
| Security, Privacy & Safety | Worked out as the requirements template says: the one-line form when nothing trips; when the only write is a draft the leader alone sees, its prohibited action ("It never sends; it only saves drafts.") and where each draft is kept; with the `Governing regime:` answer and the writes. This row is the light track's list of writes: name each write with its place, or "[place] not known yet" when the live-writes reply didn't give one. No question is asked about a place |

"Looks fine" keeps the defaults as they are: the rubber-stamp re-ask doesn't apply to this message. A correction is taken as given. If the leader wants the work done the same way every time, Part 4's method question runs ("What comes after that?" and its offers included) and the steps are played back, each recorded as `flexible`; nothing else in Part 4 is asked unless the leader switches to the full version.

Then the preview (the requirements template), the file and the close, as on the full track.

## Switching to full

- **"Make it the full version" in reply to the N-L1 message,** with item 0 unanswered: the interview is full from the start. N-D17's main form with its count, then the ready line, as on the full track (SKILL.md, *The track*); N-D23 is asked when Part 2 opens.
- **After the light items have begun** (N-L2 fires, or the leader says "make it the full version"): the switch message, and how each later part opens, are below, in *A switch to full after the light items have begun* (stated there once). No ready line, and no count at the signposts after a switch. Then the full parts run in order from Part 1. A qualifying source already known gets the policy question at the end of Part 2's opening sequence; when Part 2 has nothing else left to ask, the policy question is Part 2's one question, right after its signpost and playback line. The progress file's `Track:` becomes `full`.

"Keep it light" in reply to the ready line, with no sign of risk known, gives the light line and item 0's message in one message. "Keep it light" later on the full track, with no sign of risk known, does the reverse of a switch: the remaining light items run, and defaults fill the rest. With a sign of risk known, say N-L2 and the track stays full.

## Resuming an old light progress file

A progress file saved before this release, `Next: Light — [an item after item 0]`, with no safety answers recorded: on resume, after the resume line, the three safety questions come first, then the item it names. The count goes before the first safety question, with the first safety question's figure, and isn't repeated before that item.

## A switch to full after the light items have begun

This also sets how each later part opens once the track has turned full, on a resume too (the progress file's `Said:` line shows `switched to full`).

   **A switch to full after the light items have begun** (an answer shows a sign of risk, "keep it light" meets a known one, or the leader says "make it the full version"), stated here once:
   - **The switch message,** one message, in this order: N-D6, when it's due (the law question's yes); N-L2 where it fires; N-D17's switch form, word for word, with no number:

     > It's the most thorough conversation in the framework, because everything built later comes from what we capture here. It runs in five parts; we've already covered some of it: (1) what this is worth, (2) where it starts and ends, and a name, (3) the outcome, (4) how the work gets done, and (5) rules, sources and practice runs.

     then Part 1's signpost, Part 1's lead-in and Part 1's first message (`part-1-what-this-is-worth.md`; the light items never ask what Part 1 asks). No ready line, and no count. N-D17 is said once, the first time the track turns full, never again at a later refusal of "keep it light". The switch message comes before any other question, even when the answer that showed the risk contradicts an earlier one: then it ends with rule 10's question in place of Part 1's first message, and Part 1's first message follows the answer.
   - **Each later part** opens with N-D6 when it's due, then its signpost (no count after a switch, including on a resume: the `Said:` line shows `switched to full`); then its lead-in, only when the part still asks what the lead-in describes; then one line (model wording) playing back what earlier answers settle; then the questions those answers don't settle. Nothing already captured is asked again as a block. **A part with nothing left to ask** says its signpost and its playback line, then goes straight on to the next part in the same message.
