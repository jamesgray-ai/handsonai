# The light interview

Read when the track is light (N-L1 said light and the leader kept it). It runs in place of Parts 1 to 5. The light track captures five things, asks three safety questions, and fills defaults for everything else, shown once to correct. The files, formats, the rules for every message, the confidential check, the progress file, the preview, the close and the registry write are the same as on the full track. Every quoted line is said exactly; each question is its own message.

There are no "Part N of 5" signposts on this track, and N-D17 is never said on it. The questions it borrows keep their full-track wording, *Why* and the rest, as SKILL.md has you read them for this track.

## The light line

Right after N-L1 (and the leader keeping it light), one line (model wording) says what this shorter version covers: the goal, what it must get right, what it works from, its rules and a few practice runs, then three safety questions.

## The five things

**A sign of risk at any item.** Whenever a Context Inventory row is saved as `Confidential` or `Regulated`, or a source is `External`, or the leader says something they pasted (a document at N-D1, a real example, an input) holds client, staff or personal details, that's a sign of risk: N-L2, and the full track carries on. A row saved `Confidential` or `External` only as the safe fallback for something the leader hasn't said (SKILL.md rules 7 and 13) is no sign of risk (SKILL.md, *Light needs every one of these*). The confidential check is still said before that content is saved.

The progress file is first saved at item 0, then after each item (`Next: Light — [the goal | what it must get right | what it works from | the rules | the practice runs | the safety questions | the defaults]`).

**0. A name and a description, in one message.** Run Part 2's boundary tests yourself first (when they find several workflows, Part 2's breakdown runs). Then one message (model wording, one question) plays back the description in Part 2's shape, which carries the trigger and the deliverable, with the name you recommend and one or two others in Part 2's style, ending "What would you change or clarify?" (for illustration only: "I'd call it Newsletter Draft (or Weekly Newsletter, or Newsletter Writing), described as: after you've gathered the week's notes, drafts the newsletter, ready for you to edit and send. What would you change or clarify?"). The reply confirms the trigger, the deliverable, the name and the description together. For running work brought in, play back the `Runs today` row in the same message. The folder line (and, with a registry and no backlog node, the process line or the no-process question) follows, as Part 2 writes it, in the same message as the goal's first question. No law question here: it comes with the safety questions. In personal life, Part 2's two personal questions ("What starts it?", "What do you end up with?") come first.

**1. The goal.** The privacy line (SKILL.md), if it hasn't been said; Part 3's real-example question (on a yes, the example is pasted, read and saved as Part 3 says; what the leader started from is asked with the practice runs, item 5); the recipe line; "say it", with "Once it's ready, who gets it?" where needed; the blank questions only where nothing fills them; and the goal playback with its question and its clause (Part 3's light form). It's the goal's one confirmation on this track: the full goal isn't played back a second time.

**2. What it must get right.** "Make it checkable", then the list played back ("Here's what every [deliverable] must get right:"), each vague word already replaced inside it by a concrete version, tagged *(my suggestion)*, or *(you said)* where the leader gave one, ending "What would you change or add?". There's no separate vague-words message. Then the send-back test with its must-have line. The list is normally three to five checks; nothing the leader named is dropped.

**3. What it works from.** The typical-case question (Part 4's question 6, its first message only), then one draft of the inputs and sources (the requirements template, draft 2's sources and inputs, with the context sentence the first time), ending "What would you change or add?". A source that trips a privacy test (a `Confidential` or `Regulated` row, an `External` source) is a sign of risk: N-L2, and the full track carries on.

**4. The rules.** The always/never question (Part 4's "Is there anything it must always do, or never do, however it goes about the work?"), then the review question in its no-steps form ("Is there a point where you review or approve the work before it goes further?"), then one draft of the rules and checkpoints in the "When… it…" shape, with standing rules offered, ending "What would you change or add?". Then the standing-rules question, only from the leader's second workflow and when it applies (the requirements template, *Standing rules*).

**5. The practice runs.** Draft 4 with two or three in total: the typical case, real (with a real example, the input behind it is the typical run, and holds the Golden Example in its row); and a tricky one, proposed when the leader gave none. End it with N-D11's light form (SKILL.md), word for word. Then the real inputs, pasted now. With a real example, one line (model wording) names it, then:

**Ask:** "What did you start from for that one?"

Its answer is the typical run's input. Any other real input named but not pasted takes Part 5's paste request. If, after the leader's changes, fewer than two remain or the typical case was dropped, propose a replacement and have it kept before going on; no tricky case is required on this track. No reference is asked for per row: the Golden Example, when there is one, is the only reference. A password or key in that answer, or in any input pasted here, is replaced, where it stood, with a placeholder ("[key removed]", "[password removed]") before anything is saved, never just left out, and N-F14 is said (SKILL.md). Then the confidential check where it applies, and the inputs numbered and saved.

## The three safety questions

In this order, each as the full track words it, with its *Why*:
1. **What it sends or changes:** Part 5's live-writes question, in whichever form fits. A draft saved only where the leader alone sees it, which they can delete, isn't a sign of risk; sending, posting, or changing a record others see is.
2. **With or without the leader:** Part 5's taking-part question, stated when a checkpoint the leader performs settles it, with its conflict line.
3. **A law or regulation:** Part 2's question, with "Which one? A name or a description is fine." and its regulated line on a yes. Skipped in personal life (`Governing regime: None`).

Any answer that shows a sign of risk (a send, a change others see, running without the leader, a regulation, a "not sure" to the law question, confidential or outside content): N-L2, and the full track carries on.

## The defaults

After the safety questions, one message (model wording) lists in plain words everything you filled in, never by the file's labels (Business Objective, Baseline, Sensitivity), ending "What would you change or add?". Each row below is said, the target included (with none, say there's no target yet). A source whose details aren't known is said as not known yet, and that alone never turns the track full:

| What | Default |
|---|---|
| How the work gets done | The AI works it out each time, from the goal, the list and the rules (`Method preference: AI works it out each time`; `Path: none — the AI plans each run from the goal`) |
| Decisions | None recorded |
| When it's unsure | "When it can't confidently handle a case, it does its best and flags it for you." |
| Business Objective, Desired Outcome, Measure | From the backlog card, the document or the description; otherwise "—" |
| Baseline | `— · Unknown` |
| Target | "—" |
| Owner, Lens, Stakeholders | The leader; Individual; none, unless the card says otherwise |
| Security, Privacy & Safety | Worked out as the requirements template says: the one-line form when nothing trips; when the only write is a draft the leader alone sees, its prohibited action ("It never sends; it only saves drafts.") and where each draft is kept; with the `Governing regime:` answer and the writes |

"Looks fine" keeps the defaults as they are: the rubber-stamp re-ask doesn't apply to this message. A correction is taken as given. If the leader wants the work done the same way every time, Part 4's method question runs ("What comes after that?" and its offers included) and the steps are played back, each recorded as `flexible`; nothing else in Part 4 is asked unless the leader switches to the full version.

Then the preview (the requirements template), the file and the close, as on the full track.

## Switching to full

When N-L2 fires, or the leader says "make it the full version", the interview carries on as the full track from that point. Say N-D17 first; then the full parts run in order from Part 1, as SKILL.md's phases say. Each opens with its signpost and one line (model wording) playing back what earlier answers settle, then asks only the full track's questions those answers don't settle; a part with nothing left to ask goes straight on to the next. Nothing already captured is asked again as a block. The progress file's `Track:` becomes `full`.

"Keep it light" on the full track, with no sign of risk known, does the reverse: the remaining light items run, and defaults fill the rest. With a sign of risk known, say N-L2 and the track stays full.
