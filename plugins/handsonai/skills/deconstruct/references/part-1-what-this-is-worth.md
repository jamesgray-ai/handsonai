# Part 1: What this is worth

Read when Part 1 starts, right after its signpost. Every quoted line is said exactly.

Bridge into it: "Before we map anything, I want to know what this is worth to you, so that when it's built we can show the improvement rather than just claim it." Then capture four of the six `Value & Measurement` fields, conversationally, not as a form. One question per message, including your follow-ups: ask how a number was got (timed or estimated) in its own message, and never add a second question to the same message.

- **Business Objective**, which strategic objective this supports. On the Individual lens it may be personal ("get my Fridays back"); don't push organizational language onto it.
- **Desired Outcome**, what changes, and for whom, when this works, in the language the business would use. **Ask:** "When this works well, what's better for the business, and who notices?"
  - *Example:* "Customers get their delivery date the same day, so fewer of them call to chase."
  - *Not sure?* Say what you'd hope for; we'll put numbers on it next.

  If the answer doesn't say who notices, ask that in its own message. Value can be quality or risk, not only time saved: "better calls mean more proposals accepted" is a valid value.
- **Measure**, what gets counted: cycle time, error rate, hours, volume, rework. It quantifies Desired Outcome; it does not replace it. "Cycle time 12 days to 4 days" says nothing about what got better or for whom: if the outcome could be deleted without the measure becoming ambiguous, push once for the outcome.
- **Baseline**, today's number, and how you got it: `Measured`, `Estimated`, or `Unknown`. `Unknown` is acceptable; do not push past it. Record `Unknown — must measure before go-live` and move on. Never offer an estimate to fill the gap: an invented baseline makes a false improvement provable, which is worse than having none.

**Pre-loaded answers.** Business Objective: if the Business node has `## Objectives`, list them and ask "Which of these does this workflow serve?" on either lens; when the Analyze candidate already carries a `Business Objective`, list them and ask this in place of "Which of these…", never both in one message: "Your Analyze report tied this workflow to [objective]. Is that right, or is it the other one?"; if the report's value is Analyze's fallback ("None of the stated objectives — protects …"), ask the list question without naming it. A personal objective the student offers instead ("get my Fridays back") still wins. With neither, ask the open question. Measure: an Organizational candidate's `Success Metrics` pre-populate it, confirmed rather than asked cold, opening "Your Analyze report suggested [measures]." Play back only the measures this workflow moves directly; a broader business effect (more calls becoming proposals) stays in Desired Outcome as the reason, never a Measure. The confirm question's *Why:* "Count what this workflow changes directly, so you can tell whether it worked." Analyze captures neither Desired Outcome nor Baseline, so always ask for both, and a number quoted in the Analyze report is not a baseline the student has confirmed: ask as usual and record `Unknown` if they do not know.

**The value lever frames the Measure question.** If the candidate carried a `Value lever` and no `Success Metrics`, open Measure with the number that lever implies, then let the student redirect: *Streamline* → steps or hours per run; *Automate* → the hours a person stops spending; *Accelerate* → cycle time or throughput; *Create value* → a count of the new thing produced. When `Success Metrics` exist, they win and the lever only shapes the wording.

If the student states an aim here ("same day"), note it: the Target question at the end of Part 5 plays it back. `Target` and `Readable When` are not asked here: they describe the revised workflow, which doesn't exist yet. They come at the end of Part 5.
