# Part 1: What this is worth

Read when Part 1 starts, before its first message. Every quoted line is said exactly; each question is its own message. Part 1's signpost carries no count: N-D17 has just given one.

Say first, after the signpost and in the same message: "Before anything else, I want to know what this is worth to you, so that when it's built we can show the improvement rather than just claim it."

Settle four of the five `Value & Measurement` fields here: the Business Objective, the Desired Outcome, the Measure and the Baseline. The Target comes at the end of Part 5, since it describes the workflow once it's running. Which form Part 1 takes depends on what the Analyze report holds (`choosing-a-workflow.md`, *Prefilled*):

- **The card form:** a workflow with an Analyze detailed card that has **What changes for the business** (a backlog pick, or running work Analyze recorded). One message, then the baseline.
- **The step-by-step form:** cold entry, running work Analyze didn't record, or an earlier report's card with no "What changes for the business". Three or four short messages.

## The card form

**Message 1,** in this order: the signpost, the lead-in above, then, word for word:

> Your Analyze report ties this to your business goal: [goal]. What changes for the business: [what changes].

then N-D18 in its approved form, with its *Why* and *Not sure?* (*The Measure*, below):
- with `Success Metrics` this workflow moves directly: **Ask:** "Your Analyze report suggested [measures]. Is that what we should count?" ([measures] may name several);
- otherwise: **Ask:** "To show it worked, I'd count [measure]. Is that what we should count?", the measure framed by the value lever (*The Measure*).

**Message 2:** N-D19 (*The Baseline*), word for word, once per kept measure.

- **[goal]** is the card's **Goal**, a registry goal. On the Organizational lens, a named Business Objective is recorded in the Value table (`choosing-a-workflow.md`), but [goal] stays the Goal, so the leader hears one thing. "Business goal" is the business's goal; the workflow's own goal, in Part 3, is "your goal".
- **[what changes]** is the card's "What changes for the business", as written there.
- **Recorded:** the Business Objective as `choosing-a-workflow.md` says; the Desired Outcome from "What changes for the business", tagged *(from your Analyze report)*. A correction to the business goal or the outcome is taken from the reply to message 1. Nothing else is asked about either: no objective question, no Desired Outcome playback.

**Fallbacks, with Goal "None named":** message 1 drops its first sentence ("Your Analyze report ties this to your business goal: [goal].").
- **With a Business node's `## Objectives`:** message 1 is the signpost, the lead-in, the objectives listed, and **Ask:** "Which of these does this workflow serve?", and it ends there. The next message opens with "What changes for the business: [what changes]." and asks N-D18. Then N-D19.
- **With none:** the Business Objective is "—", and message 1 is the signpost, the lead-in, "What changes for the business: [what changes]." and N-D18.

## The step-by-step form

In this order, one question per message: the Business Objective (asked only when something is pre-loaded), the Desired Outcome (drafted for the leader to correct), the Measure and the Baseline.

### 1. Business Objective

Which strategic objective this supports. Ask only when something is pre-loaded:

- **A Business node's `## Objectives`, on cold entry or when the card names no goal:** list them, then **Ask:** "Which of these does this workflow serve?"
- **A backlog pick whose card has a Business Objective but no Goal row** (an earlier report): ask this instead, never both in one message: **Ask:** "Your Analyze report tied this workflow to [objective]. Is that right, or is it the other one?" (with three or more objectives listed, "…or is it another one?"). Cold entry never had a candidate objective to confirm.
- **The candidate's value is one of Analyze's fallbacks** ("None of the stated objectives — protects …" or "No stated objectives — protects [what]"): the list question, without naming it.

A personal objective the leader offers instead ("get my Fridays back") still wins. With nothing pre-loaded, ask nothing: record the objective from the leader's description or document where it names one, otherwise "—". On the Individual lens it may be personal; don't push organizational language onto it.

### 2. Desired Outcome

What changes, and for whom, in the language the business uses. Never ask it open: draft it from what's known (the backlog card, the cold-entry answer, the leader's document), tag it (*(you said)*, *(from your [document])* or *(my suggestion)*), and play it back in one line that names what gets better and who notices, ending "What would you change or clarify?" (for illustration only; use the leader's own case: "Customers get their delivery date the same day, so fewer of them call to chase *(my suggestion)*. What would you change or clarify?"). The rubber-stamp guard applies to it. Value can be quality or risk, not only time saved: "better calls mean more proposals accepted" is a valid value.

## The Measure

What gets counted (cycle time, error rate, hours, volume, rework). It quantifies the Desired Outcome and never replaces it, and it counts only what this workflow moves directly: a broader business effect stays in the Desired Outcome as the reason, never a Measure. Confirm it, never ask it cold (N-D18, in both forms):

- **An Organizational candidate's `Success Metrics`,** played back only where this workflow moves them directly: **Ask:** "Your Analyze report suggested [measures]. Is that what we should count?"
- **Otherwise,** a measure you propose: **Ask:** "To show it worked, I'd count [measure]. Is that what we should count?"
- *Why:* Count what this workflow changes directly, so you can tell whether it worked.
- *Not sure?* Say yes. You can change it later.

**The value lever frames the proposal** when the candidate carries a `Value lever` and no `Success Metrics`: *Streamline*, steps or hours per run; *Automate*, the hours a person stops spending; *Accelerate*, cycle time or throughput; *Create value*, a count of the new thing produced. With no lever (cold entry), propose from the Desired Outcome and the work as described. `Success Metrics`, when present, win, and the lever only shapes the wording.

## The Baseline

Today's number for the Measure. Always ask it, on every form: Analyze doesn't capture it, and a number in the opportunity report isn't a baseline the leader has confirmed.

**Ask:** "What's [the measure, in plain words] today?" (for illustration only; use the leader's own measure: "What's the time each month-end reconciliation takes today?")
- *Not sure?* Say you don't know. Leaving it unknown is fine.

Read how it was got from the answer; never ask it. `Measured` when the leader names where the number comes from (a report, a log, a count); `Estimated` otherwise; `Unknown` when they don't know, which a "not sure" records at once (with no number, the row reads `— · Unknown`). Never push past `Unknown`, and never offer an estimate to fill the gap: an invented baseline makes a false improvement provable.

## An aim

An aim the leader states here ("same day") is noted for the target question at the end of Part 5. Only one they said in their own words counts: an aim only the Analyze report or the backlog card states ("Clients hear back the same day"), which the leader agreed to as part of a playback or a measure, isn't theirs, so the target question takes its no-aim form.

## Never here

The policy question is never asked inside Part 1, even when a reply here shows a source that needs it: it waits for the end of Part 2's opening sequence (`part-2-scope-and-a-name.md`, *The policy question*).
