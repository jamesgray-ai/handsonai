# Part 4: How the work gets done

Read when Part 4 starts, right after its signpost; on the light track, read it for the few questions that track borrows (the typical case, always/never, the review question's no-steps form, and the method question if the leader wants the work done the same way). Every quoted line is said exactly; each question is its own message unless a section says "in one message". `[deliverable]`, `[case]` and `[input]` are the leader's own ("the follow-up email", "a scoping call", "your call notes").

Say first: "Next, we'll walk through how this work gets done and mark what must always happen the same way."

This part draws out what only the leader knows about how the work should go: what must always happen the same way, where a person steps in, which decisions exist and what settles them, how much the inputs vary, and what expertise matters. The steps are a requirement only when the leader wants the work done the same way; otherwise they're how these things are drawn out. Design uses it to decide how much the AI works out on its own; Deconstruct never decides that and never names a level. After the playback every leader gets the same shared questions; the only differences are the cells of the matrix at the end.

## 1. Consistency

**Ask:** "Should it go about the work the same way every time, or can the AI work out how to do it each time, from your goal and your list?"
- *Why:* Same way every time means we write down the steps. If the AI works it out, we write down only what it must always and never do.
- *Not sure?* Say the same way; you can loosen it later.

Record the answer as the first line of `## Path`: `Method preference: same way every time | AI works it out each time | some of each`. It decides only how the path is drafted (section 2). It's a preference, never a level.

If a leader who chose "AI works it out" then names steps that must always happen in order, say: "Those steps sound fixed, so I'll note this as some of each: those steps stay the same, and the AI works out the rest. Does that match?" On a yes: the preference becomes "some of each"; place the named steps in the order the leader gave, with `- The AI plans this part` where their answers put it (when that isn't clear, **Ask:** "Where does the AI's part go: before, between or after these steps?"); give the fixed-and-flexible definition (question 2) on its first use; play the steps back (section 3); give the fixed-step reasons (question 2's one message); and run questions 1 and 3 for those steps. Don't ask question 2's pick again: the named steps are fixed. From then on, follow the "some of each" column of the matrix.

## 2. Draft the path

### Same way every time

**Ask:** "If you handed [the deliverable] to a skilled colleague, how would you want them to go about it?"
- *Why:* Your method tells the AI which steps to follow exactly and which it can work out for itself.
- *Example:* "First I check the order, then I confirm stock, then I send the customer the delivery date."
- *Not sure?* Walk me through how you did the last one.

On cold entry, play the cold-entry answer back here as the method, one line per step, ending "What would you change or clarify?", in place of this question; play a document's steps (an SOP) back the same way. For running work brought in, play back the answer to how it runs today the same way. In a personal-life registry, the personal-life wording replaces the question.

**An answer stops early unless it reaches the end Part 2 set** (the deliverable sent, handed over or filed). After the method answer, in its own message, **Ask:** "What comes after that?", repeated until the leader says that's all, says they don't know, or the answer reaches that end. When it isn't clear whether it has, or an earlier answer already covers the rest (say that part first), ask once instead: **Ask:** "Is that the end, or does anything happen after you [send / file / hand over] it?", using the verb for how Part 2 said the work ends. If the method stops partway or the leader doesn't know what comes next:

**Ask:** "Want me to propose the rest, and you correct it?"
- *Not sure?* Say yes, and correct what I propose.

With no method at all, or a leader who'd rather not describe it:

**Ask:** "Want me to propose the steps, and you correct them?"
- *Not sure?* Say yes, and correct what I propose.

Propose from Parts 1 to 3 and the backlog candidate. Only the steps you add carry `· proposed by AI`; when you propose the whole path, also write the line `Path: proposed by AI`. There's no step-count threshold.

### AI works it out each time

No steps are written down and no sample plan is shown: the AI plans fresh on every run, and a guessed plan would lead the leader.

**Ask:** "Is there anything it must always do, or never do, however it goes about the work?"
- *Example:* "Always use this week's price list; never quote a discount."
- *Not sure?* Say no; we'll cover rules in Part 5.

Each answer is a rule ("It always…", "It never…"), an R line in the file. `## Path` holds `Path: none — the AI plans each run from the goal` in place of step lines. On cold entry, the cold-entry answer isn't played back as a path; offer any "It always…" or "It never…" rule it holds in this question. For the AI to work it out, it's given a clear goal (Part 3) and its limits (this question, the shared questions and Part 5); never guess the plan.

### Some of each

Run the same-way method question, "What comes after that?" and its offers in full; then the playback (section 3); then question 1 (who does it). Then question 2 is, word for word:

**Ask:** "Which of these must always happen exactly that way? The AI will work out the rest each time."

Then the fixed-step reasons; then the always/never question above, for the AI-planned part; then questions 3 to 8.

In the file, each run of unpicked AI steps becomes one `- The AI plans this part` line at its place; unpicked steps a person does stay as flexible steps with their role tag, so a checkpoint's `Where` still names them. Once question 2 has run, don't call unpicked AI steps by number again: any reference to one (a decision's step, a write, a context item's `Used By`) names the AI's part instead ("the AI's part after Step 2").

**All or none picked.** If every AI step is picked, say "Every step you picked stays fixed, so I'll note this as the same way every time." then "Does that match?"; follow the "same way" column from there, except that the today question isn't asked after this switch (the steps are already confirmed, and the leader may want them done differently now). If no step is picked, say "None of these needs to stay fixed, so I'll note this as the AI working it out each time." then "Does that match?"; each step a person does becomes a rule with its role tag ("When the brief is ready, you add your questions to it. · (you)"), play the rules back, and follow the "AI works it out" column. On a no to either switch, ask question 2's line once more; a second all-or-none pick is recorded as given and the switch applied without asking again.

## 3. The playback

One playback, the same shape for everyone, in plain words, ending "What would you change or clarify?":

- **With steps:** "Here are the steps:" then one line per step, then any "always" and "never" rules already given.
- **With no steps:** "Here's what it must always and never do:" then those rules. With none, leave the playback out.
- **Same way every time, when the work is done today:** one message, word for word:

  **Ask:** "Here are your steps: [numbered steps]. Is this how you do it today, or how you'd like it done from now on?"
  - *Why:* If today's way is different, I'll check which of today's habits should stay.

  A correction to the steps in the reply counts as the change-or-clarify answer. When today's way differs, the path records how the leader wants it done. A reply describing today's way that ran past a short paragraph is first played back in plain words, one line per step of today's way (not as the path), ending "What would you change or clarify?"; then, one difference per message, **Ask:** "Today you also [step or workaround]. Should that stay?" A step or workaround they drop isn't a step, and nothing produces or uses it.

Any answer that ran past a short paragraph is played back this way before another question; never mention the threshold. As you play the steps back, tag each step's `uses` and `writes` from the answers; C and W numbers are given only when written to the file.

## 4. The shared questions

In this order, each in its own message unless marked. With no steps, a step question is asked against the rules or a moment ("before anything is sent", "after the draft"), or left out without comment when there's nothing to ask it about. The matrix lists what runs in each branch.

### Question 1, who does it

**Ask:** "Once this is built, which of these steps will you, or someone else, still do yourselves?"
- *Why:* Steps a person does stay with them; the AI does the rest.
- *Not sure?* Name the ones you'd want to keep doing yourself.

For each step named without who does it: **Ask:** "Who does [step]?" The answer is the step's role tag: `(you)` for the leader's own steps, even when they give their job title, or the role, such as `(finance team)`, meaning a person still performs the step in the new workflow. Untagged steps are AI steps. When a step moves to the AI, rename it for the AI's action ("Read the call notes") and play the rename back.

With no steps: **Ask:** "Is there any part you, or someone else, will always do yourselves?" Each part named becomes a rule with its role tag ("When the brief is ready, you add your questions to it. · (you)").

### Question 2, must stay (steps only)

With no steps, the always/never question has already done this job; don't ask it twice. On its first use, say: "**Fixed** means a step always happens exactly as written, in this order. **Flexible** means it can be adapted to fit each case. A step you do yourself can be fixed too." Then:

**Ask:** "Which of these steps must always happen exactly that way?" ("Some of each" uses its own line, section 2.)
- *Not sure?* Leave it flexible. You can fix a step later.

Then the reasons, for every fixed step in one message, each with a suggestion to keep or change: "Here's why I think each one is fixed; keep or change: Step [N], [name]: [your own call | required by a regulation | required by company policy] *(my suggestion)* …", ending "What would you change or add?" With one fixed step:

**Ask:** "Is that required, by a regulation or company policy, or your own call?"
- *Why:* Required steps are never changed; your own calls can be revisited later.

A "required" answer that doesn't say which gets one follow-up naming regulation or policy. The step line records `fixed (regulation)`, `fixed (policy)` or `fixed (your judgment)`, never anything else, and a reason you chose is always tagged *(my suggestion)*. When the leader isn't sure of a reason and hasn't called the step required, the default is `fixed (your judgment)`; a step called required whose source stays unknown is `fixed (policy)` by default. A proposed step the leader marks fixed takes its reason from this message and loses its `· proposed by AI` tag; "none" leaves every step flexible with its tag. With any step fixed, a `Path: proposed by AI` line stays, and the fixed steps show which are the leader's.

### Question 3, review or approve

A person's step that is itself a review, check or approval is stated, not asked: "Step [N], your review, becomes a checkpoint: the workflow stops there until you've approved the [deliverable]." Ask only about other person steps that could be a review point (not ones that only act, such as sending, filing or booking):

- one such step: **Ask:** "Is [step] a point where you review or approve the work before it goes further?"
- several, in one message: **Ask:** "Are any of these points where you review or approve the work before it goes further: [steps]?"
- no steps, when the leader named a part a person does: the one-step form naming that part ("Is reading the brief and adding your questions a point where you review or approve the work before it goes further?");
- no steps and no such part, or steps but no person step that could be a review point and no review step already stated: **Ask:** "Is there a point where you review or approve the work before it goes further?"

With the first of these that's asked:
- *Why:* Review and approval points become checkpoints the workflow can't skip, and testing checks each one.
- *Not sure?* Say yes; you can remove it later.

A yes adds a checkpoint (a G line), its `Where` naming the step or the moment, in the "When… it…" shape. Checkpoints are asked about here; Part 5 adds one only from the taking-part and when-unsure answers.

### Question 4, decisions

**Ask:** "Does the work change depending on the [case] — for example, a different next step for different kinds of customer?"
- *Why:* This shows whether the work follows your rules or needs judgment along the way.
- *Example:* "An expense over $500 goes to my manager; under that, I approve it myself."
- *Not sure?* Say no; the tricky case we'll look at shortly often shows one.

For each decision: **Ask:** "What decides which way it goes?", even when the leader already named the condition, since its answer is what settles the decision. Each Decision line records what settles it: `a rule on a value`, `your criteria` or `open judgment`. `a rule on a value` is only for a number, date, field or category stated in the input ("over $50k goes to the controller"), whoever supplies it; classifying from free text (who's buying, from call notes) is `your criteria` or `open judgment`. When unclear, ask rather than choose:

**Ask:** "Could a fixed rule settle this, does it follow criteria you could write down, or does it take judgment each time?"
- *Why:* This shows how much of the choice the AI makes on its own.
- *Not sure?* Say judgment. You can tighten it later.

When the AI reads the value from the input the workflow works on (an amount on a submitted report), the line adds `· value read by AI`; where the value comes from is a follow-up when the answers don't say. Asked in every branch: decisions are the strongest signal Design has.

### Question 5, regulated work

Only when Part 2's law answer was yes, right after the decisions, each in its own message:

**Ask:** "Which steps does the regulation require?" (only about steps not already `fixed (regulation)`; with a proposed path, about the proposed steps)
- *Not sure?* Name the ones you're sure of; the rest stay flexible.

Those steps become `fixed (regulation)`. Then:

**Ask:** "Which decisions must a person make or approve?"
- *Why:* Those become checkpoints, so the AI never makes them alone.

A decision already has a checkpoint when one sits at its step or moment, or later, before anything leaves the workflow; skip such decisions without saying so, and leave the question out when every decision has one. Each decision named becomes a checkpoint.

**With no steps** ("AI works it out"), the first question is instead:

**Ask:** "What does the regulation require it to do, every time?"

Each answer becomes a `fixed (regulation)` step, and say: "Because the rules set some steps, those stay fixed; the AI works out the rest." The preference becomes "some of each": `Path: none` gives way to those steps, with `- The AI plans this part` where the answers put it (when unclear, the "Where does the AI's part go" question); play the steps back; give the fixed definition if question 2 hasn't run; run questions 1 and 3 for those steps, playing back any answers already given against rules or moments and asking only the open part. If the leader names no required step, it stays "AI works it out". Then the second regulation question.

### Question 6, the range

**Ask:** "Think of a typical [case]. What would [the input] cover?"
- *Why:* Your typical and tricky [cases] become the practice runs used to test the workflow; you'll paste real ones near the end.
- *Not sure?* One sentence is enough; you'll paste real notes later.

Then, in its own message:

**Ask:** "And a tricky one: a [case] where it would be easy to get the [deliverable] wrong?"
- *Why:* Workflows fail on the tricky cases, so one of the practice runs uses this.
- *Example:* "The order was split across two warehouses."

In that question only [case] and [deliverable] change: "get the [deliverable] wrong" stays as written, even where another verb would suit the case (never "pad the newsletter").

When nothing comes in yet: **Ask:** "Where will the information come from?" Then, separately, **Ask:** "Does it exist yet?"

Draft the Inputs lines (`I1`…) from the trigger and these answers, tagged *(my suggestion)*, for Part 5's second draft. A source that doesn't exist yet is recorded in Inputs as `Status: not available yet`.

### Question 7, expertise

**Ask:** "What would a capable beginner get wrong in a [deliverable] that you wouldn't?"
- *Why:* What you'd catch that a beginner wouldn't becomes a rule the AI follows every time.
- *Example:* a sommelier might say: "Pairing by grape instead of by the dish's sauce."

An answer about a step goes on that step's detail block, as its Expertise line; with no steps, it's a rule. An answer that's a quality standard ("lead with a hypothesis") is held and offered as a check in Part 5's third draft, *(you said)*, unless the list already covers it.

### Question 8, detail only where it matters

A rule from the always/never or who-does questions needs no detail question: it's already a rule. A step gets detail (a rule, a check, the expertise) only when it carries order that matters, a rule or standard, a check or handoff, an outside action, or the leader's expertise; everything else stays one line.

**Count first,** from every answer so far, before any detail question; never say the count, and never ask one step's detail question before counting.

- **The detail table,** when the law answer is yes or four or more steps need detail (never say the threshold): one table, opened "Here's what each step must get right. Rules are what it must follow; checks are where someone confirms the work. Keep or change each of my suggestions.", with columns Step · Rule · Check, each rule tagged *(you said)* or *(my suggestion)* for a keep or drop, ending "What would you change or add?"; never one question per step, and never a rule or threshold stated as fact.
- **Otherwise,** for each step that needs detail, in its own message:

  **Ask:** "Does [step] have a rule it must follow, or something that must be checked before the next step?"
  - *Why:* Rules and checks are what the AI must get right every time, so they're written down for that step.
  - *Not sure?* Say no. Testing will show if it needs one.

## The close of Part 4

Once the last answer is in (a keep-or-drop re-ask included), say, as its own line before Part 5's signpost: "That's how the work gets done. Next: the rules it follows, the sources it uses, and the cases we'll test it on."

## The question matrix

*req*: always asked; *cond*: asked when its condition holds; *n/a*: not in this branch. After a switch (a "some of each" pick of all or none, a named fixed step, or a regulation with no steps), the new branch's column applies from that point.

| Question | Same way every time | AI works it out each time | Some of each |
|---|---|---|---|
| Method, "What comes after that?", the offers | req (cond for the offers) | n/a | req (cond for the offers) |
| Always/never | n/a | req | req (AI-planned part) |
| Playback | req (the today form when done today) | cond (when there are "always" or "never" rules) | req |
| Today question | cond (done today; inside the playback; never after a switch) | n/a | n/a |
| 1 Who does it | req | req (no-steps form) | req |
| 2 Must stay, and reasons | req | n/a | req (its own line) |
| 3 Review or approve | req (stated, asked, or grouped) | req (no-steps form, or the one-step form naming a part a person does) | req (stated, asked, or grouped) |
| 4 Decisions | req | req | req |
| 5 Regulated work | cond (law answer yes) | cond (no-steps form) | cond (law answer yes) |
| 6 Range, typical and tricky | req | req | req |
| 7 Expertise | req | req | req |
| 8 Detail | cond (steps that carry detail) | n/a | cond (steps that carry detail) |
