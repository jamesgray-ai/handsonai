# The catalog and registering the candidates

Read before the list (fast path: before the report), for a workflow with no process and, on the team lens, team work in another team's process; at the list, the catalog confirmation (N-A15); at registration; and at the entry check for a `backlog.md` beside a registry.

**A workflow with no process** (registry present; otherwise candidates stay grouped by area of work). Before the list (fast path: before the report), check every candidate has a Process; an item under "**Not in a process yet**" is placed here. Where none fits, teach the term and ask, once per new process, exactly:

**Ask:** "[Workflow] doesn't fit any of your processes. What would you call the set of related work it belongs to, and which function (the team or role that owns that kind of work) looks after it? Your functions are [the registry's Function names]."
- *Why:* every workflow sits under one process, and the process's owner owns the workflow too.
- *Example:* a bookkeeper might say: "Client Onboarding, and Client Services looks after it."
- *Not sure?* I'll suggest a name from your own words and the function in your registry that fits it best. You can change either later.

A person, or a function not in the registry, maps to the Function that fits, said in one line ("I'll put it under [Function]."); if none fits, **Ask:** "Shall I add [function] as a new function?" The answers are recorded in the progress file; nothing is written to the registry yet. **When a new process is written:** after the list is confirmed, and only if a kept workflow sits under it (a new process whose workflows were all left out or moved is never written). Then write a complete minimal Process node per `naming-workflows` (never a default or ownerless stub), with its line in the right Line of Business's `# Processes` list and `registry/processes/index.md`; a new function agreed for it, written at the same time and only then, is a complete Function node per `registry/SCHEMA.md` (title, description, one sentence of charter prose, its GENERATED `# Owns` block) with its line in `registry/functions/index.md`. Never write an `owner:` matching no Function file. **The owner's `# Owns`:** writing a Process, write it into its owner Function's `# Owns` block yourself, in the form the indexing-registry skill gives (*Regenerate GENERATED blocks*: one bullet per Process the Function owns, in path order), so the close's check finds it current. The catalog shows a new process with "(new)". **Its line of business:** the one it plainly belongs to; when it fits more than one (work that spans the whole business, such as finance or admin), put it under the line it serves most, last in that line's list, and say so in one line, exactly, with no question: "I'll put [process] under [line of business]; say if it belongs elsewhere." The registry has no cross-business home for a process (every Process sits in exactly one line's list), so never invent one. Ask which line only when it fits none.

**Team work in another team's process** (the team form of the organizational lens only; `the-walk.md`, *The team's own work*). Before the list, when a kept candidate is the team's own work but sits in a process another function owns, ask once, for all such candidates together, in its own message:

**Ask:** "[Workflow] is your team's work, but it sits in [process], which [function] owns. Keep it there, or put it under one of your team's processes or a new one?" *(your words)* (several: "[Workflow] and [Workflow] are your team's work, but they sit in processes other teams own: [process] ([function]) and [process] ([function]). Keep them there, or put them under your team's processes or new ones?" *(your words)*)
- *Why:* every workflow sits under one process, and the process's owner owns the workflow too.
- *Not sure?* Say 'keep it there.' You can move it later.

[Workflow] is the short name the student last heard for that work (the shortlist's short name, or the name the playback used), never a workflow name they haven't seen yet: the list, with its workflow names, comes after. It counts as one of the run's two follow-ups (`the-walk.md`), taking the place of one not used, so the run stays within its question count. With both follow-ups already used, ask nothing: the candidate stays where it is, as on "keep it there", and the student can still move it at the list.

"Keep it there": it stays under that process, owned by its owner. One of the team's processes: move it there. A new one: *A workflow with no process*, above, with the team's own functions offered. Whatever is chosen, the list's "owned by that process's owner" stays true. Never move such a candidate without this question, and never ask it on another lens.

**The list: one confirmation of the named list (N-A15)**, the student's one look before anything is written. Place each candidate under the Process where it was found, unless it clearly belongs elsewhere (it happens before that Process starts, or another Process's owner does it): then move it, with the reason in brackets after its description. On the team lens, a candidate of the team's own work in another team's process is placed by the question above, never moved by this rule. Every process with workflows, in registry order, numbered straight through (a later or other-lens run: only this run's, 1 to N; the report continues its own numbering), each as name, a one-sentence description of about 20 words in the `naming-workflows` shape (one sentence: when it starts, what it produces, who receives or acts on it; no targets, no tool names; a purpose or reason ("so clients hear from you…") only in the student's own words, never one you added), and its value lever, with "(already runs)" after work that already runs (a correction to one, such as "that one isn't in use", is taken here as `evidence-pass.md`, *Work that already runs*, says: it becomes a candidate again). Where a trigger or deliverable is "Not clear yet", the description says so and still reads naturally ("…a draft post; what starts it is still open"; "When a client signs, the team starts onboarding; what it produces is still open"); never invent one. No size and no ranking. A registry workflow the evidence pass recorded as running goes on one line, unnumbered: "[Workflow] is already in your registry, now recorded as running". No registry: open with "Here's your list of workflows.", drop the owner sentence, and the *Why* is "the name and description are what Deconstruct starts from later." Say exactly this, with the student's workflows:

> Here's what goes into your registry. Each workflow sits under the process it belongs to and is owned by that process's owner.
>
> **Process:** [Process]
>
> 1. **[Workflow name].** [One-sentence description.] Value lever: [Streamline / Automate / Accelerate / Create value].
> 2. …

Then the value-lever definition line: "Each workflow names its **value lever**: the kind of value AI creates here — Streamline, Automate, Accelerate, or Create value." *(your words)* Then, when shortlist items are still not shown (`evidence-pass.md`, *The answer*) and the student didn't say "that's enough", N-A19's "[n] more…" line for them, once, in its form. Then:

**Ask:** "That's [k], enough to choose from. Anything to add, rename, reword, move, or leave out before I save them?" *(your words)*
- *Why:* the name and description are what you'll see on your dashboard, and what Deconstruct starts from later.
- *Not sure?* Say "looks good." You can edit any workflow later.

[k] counts the numbered workflows. With fewer than 3, the question starts at "Anything…": "Anything to add, rename, reword, move, or leave out before I save them?" *(your words)*

**A workflow added here** is placed (*A workflow with no process*, above), gets one of the open question's two follow-ups if any is left (`the-walk.md`), and is said back in one line before saving.

**"Show the rest" at the list** brings those items as the shortlist does (`evidence-pass.md`, ending with N-A13); the items kept join the list, which is shown again with N-A15. Items still not shown at the close are named in the report's **Sources**.

**After the answer:** apply every change, read everything the save needs, say the saving line once (SKILL.md: "Saving your report and workflows now; this takes a moment."; not when every workflow was left out), then, with no other text until the close, write the report (`report-format.md`) and the nodes (no registry: `backlog.md`) in one batch, as SKILL.md's *After the list is confirmed* says. A left-out workflow keeps its card, marked "Left out of the backlog", and gets no registry entry and no backlog line. The close names the report; there's no separate message about it.

**Register the candidates (registry present)** as backlog Workflow nodes under their confirmed Process, in the registry (`registry/` in the working folder; on the web, through the connection), as the indexing-registry skill's `registry-bundle.md` says. Work that already runs gets `state: in-production` instead, never the backlog, and its skill or agent under `# Skills` or `# Agents`: a link when the file is in this workspace, else its name as plain text, with where it lives only when you saw it (`evidence-pass.md`, *The name and home*): "Newsletter Repurposer, a skill in your Claude account", or just its name as the student gave it.

   ```yaml
   ---
   type: Workflow
   title: "[Workflow name]"
   description: "[One sentence, naming-workflows shape: when it starts, what it produces, who receives or acts on it.]"
   generated: { by: process:analyze, at: YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ }
   state: backlog
   trigger: "[Trigger field]"
   ---
   # [Workflow name]

   [Description.]

   # Artifacts

   - [Opportunity report]([the report's link])
   [- [SOP](../../sops/[file].md), when the candidate came from the student's own SOP]

   # Skills

   [For work that already runs: the skill or agent as the leader named it, and where it lives only when that was seen ("auditing-agent-skills, in the Claude account"; otherwise the name alone). A page never calls the leader "the user"; write the leader's words, or name the platform.]

   # Agents

   # Insights

   <!-- GENERATED:insights -->
   <!-- /GENERATED -->
   ```

   The report's link is file-relative from the node, `../../framework/ai-opportunity-report.md`, on every platform (`registry-bundle.md`, rule 4). Write only this link; an older node's link in another form is left as it is (it still resolves). The body repeats `description` on purpose. `trigger` is provisional. No `execution_mode` or `autonomy` (Design sets those); pain point, goal and value lever stay in the report.
   **A candidate from the student's own SOP** (its evidence is a file in `sops/`): link that SOP under `# Artifacts` as `- [SOP](../../sops/[file].md)`, the link form the registry uses for SOPs, so the registry's check finds it claimed. One SOP goes to one Workflow node, the best match; never link an SOP another node already links, or one whose `workflow:` field names another workflow. A process guide in `process-guides/` is never linked this way (the check doesn't require it).
2. Add each stub's line to its Process's `# Workflows` list and to `registry/workflows/index.md`, replacing any placeholder line there (`_No workflows captured yet._`, `_No entries yet…_`) (bundle-root-relative link: `[Weekly Status Report](/workflows/weekly-status-report.md)`). If a node for that slug, or for the same work under another name, already exists, merge — never overwrite fields already set, except the backlog-to-`in-production` move of `state` the evidence pass confirmed. Keep every key on an existing node you don't own (`registry-bundle.md`, rule 10).

**The backlog with no registry** (locally and on the web): write `backlog.md` in the framework folder: a `# Backlog` heading, then a `## [Area of work]` heading per area in the report's order, each candidate as `[n]. **[Workflow name].** [One-sentence description.]` in the report's numbering. It holds only the candidates the list kept: never a left-out workflow, and never work that already runs, which stays in the report. A later run, on the same lens or the other one, adds its candidates under their areas, continuing the numbering. Never tell the student the file's name; speak of the report and the backlog.

**A `backlog.md` beside a registry** (Analyze's entry check 3: the registry exists, and `backlog.md` is still in the framework folder from before it was set up). Once a registry exists, its `backlog` nodes are the backlog, and nothing else reads `backlog.md`, so its candidates would drop out. Offer, in one line in your own words, to add the workflows it lists to the registry's backlog, naming how many. On yes:
1. Read the registry (`part-1-reading-and-the-lens.md`, *Reading what's known*) and the report the candidates came from.
2. Place each candidate under the Process that fits it, asking *A workflow with no process* where none does.
3. Show the list with these workflows, in `backlog.md`'s order, asking N-A15's "Anything…" form ("Anything to add, rename, reword, move, or leave out before I save them?" *(your words)*), and apply the changes.
4. Write each one's stub and index lines as above (a candidate already in the registry by what it is: merge, as above), with the report linked under `# Artifacts`, then the maintenance pass.
5. Move `backlog.md` into the framework folder's `history/` as `backlog-YYYY-MM-DD-HHMM.md` (locally and on a drive; never delete it), so it isn't offered again.
6. Say in one line how many were added, then carry on with the opening.

On no, leave `backlog.md` where it is, say in one line that those workflows stay out of the registry's backlog until they're added, and carry on with the opening.
