# The open question, follow-ups and the walk

Read before the opening when there's nothing to scan (the opening then ends with N-A14's opening form), and otherwise when the evidence pass ends. It covers the open question (N-A14), playback and follow-ups (N-A22, N-A20, the do-more-of question), the offer and the walk when there are fewer than 3 candidates (N-A17), and *Thin answers*.

A **candidate** is a kept shortlist item, or a piece of work the student names with a problem or with value in doing more of it. Work recorded as running never counts toward the 3.

## The open question (N-A14)

Asked once on every run, whatever the shortlist kept. When it has already been shown in this chat and is still unanswered (the resume message showed it, then the student said "show the rest"), repeat only its question sentence, not the whole block: no "Think of the last few weeks…" sentence, no "Two or three is plenty…", no *Why* or *Not sure?* line. The question sentence is the form's second sentence, as the table gives it: "Where did time go, or things slip, that you'd rather hadn't?" (business or company, personal life, no registry); "Where did [unit name]'s time go, or things slip, that you'd rather hadn't?" (unit); "Where did your team's time go, or things slip, that you'd rather hadn't?" (team); "What took your time, or slipped, that you'd hand to someone else tomorrow?" (individual). Never precede it with a list of likely pain points (students echo the list). Say the form for the lens and scope, in your own words, ending with "Two or three is plenty; 'not sure' is fine too.":

| Lens and scope | N-A14 |
|---|---|
| Organizational, business or company | "Think of the last few weeks across [lines of business / your company's work], including anything that only comes around now and then, like a launch. Where did time go, or things slip, that you'd rather hadn't? Two or three is plenty; 'not sure' is fine too." *(your words)* |
| Organizational, unit | "Think of the last few weeks across [unit name]'s work, including anything that only comes around now and then, like a launch. Where did [unit name]'s time go, or things slip, that you'd rather hadn't? Two or three is plenty; 'not sure' is fine too." *(your words)* |
| Organizational, team | "Think of the last few weeks, including anything that only comes around now and then. Where did your team's time go, or things slip, that you'd rather hadn't? Two or three is plenty; 'not sure' is fine too." *(your words)* |
| Individual | "Think of the last few weeks, including anything that only comes around now and then. What took your time, or slipped, that you'd hand to someone else tomorrow? Two or three is plenty; 'not sure' is fine too." *(your words)* |
| Personal life | "Think of the last few weeks in [areas]. Where did time go, or things slip, that you'd rather hadn't? Two or three is plenty; 'not sure' is fine too." *(your words)* |
| No registry | "Think of the last few weeks in your work, including anything that only comes around now and then. Where did time go, or things slip, that you'd rather hadn't? Two or three is plenty; 'not sure' is fine too." *(your words)* |

[lines of business] names the Business node's lines of business, in at most four words ("consulting and training"); "your company's work" is for the company or unit the owner leads, with no unit name. [unit name] comes from the Business node.

- *Why:* The places where time or results are lost are where a workflow is worth building.
- *Not sure?* Tell me one task you'd hand to someone else tomorrow if you could. Or attach a document that shows how the work goes, and I'll read just that.

**Its forms:**
- **After a scan that kept anything,** it begins "Beyond those, …" (two or more kept) or "Beyond that, …" (one kept), with the form's first word in lower case ("Beyond those, think of the last few weeks…"; "Beyond that, think of the last few weeks…").
- **The plain form,** as the table gives it: after a declined scan, a keep of none, nothing fits, or everything already registered.
- **The opening form,** when there's nothing to scan (`evidence-pass.md`): it ends the opening, and after the question says "You can also attach a document that shows the work." *(your words)* in place of the *Why*. Give the *Why* if the student asks why.

A document attached in answer is read as `evidence-pass.md` says (*A document attached mid-flow*); it adds no question.

## Playback, filing and follow-ups

**Playback.** Play the student's words back, "So far I have: …", naming each piece of work and filing it under its process, with new processes marked "(new)". Several pieces are played back as separate pieces ("That's three pieces of work: launch emails, waitlist follow-up, and promotion across channels."). A piece becomes a candidate only when the student names a problem with it, or value in doing more of it. Tie each to the registry goal it serves (the first in the Business node's order when it serves several), by the goal map (`part-1-reading-and-the-lens.md`, *Which process serves which goal*), or "None named". The tie is said in the playback: "It goes under [process], which serves your goal: [goal]." *(your words)* ([goal] as written, without its final full stop; with several pieces, one sentence per piece, "[Piece] goes under [process], which serves your goal: [goal]." *(your words)*; for a process that serves no goal, "It goes under [process]." *(your words)*). A piece that matches a shortlist item not shown yet (`evidence-pass.md`, *The answer*) takes that item's evidence clause and problem, with the student's words added, rather than being filed as new.

**Follow-ups:** at most two in a run, across all pieces, one per piece, only for what's missing, one thing per message (on the team lens, N-A26 at the list takes the place of one left unused: `registering-candidates.md`, *Team work in another team's process*):
1. the problem first, N-A22:

   **Ask:** "What's the problem with [piece] today?" *(your words)*
2. then the trigger, N-A20:

   **Ask:** "What usually kicks it off: something arriving, a request, or a set day?" *(your words)*

Pieces past the second follow-up, and anything still fuzzy, are "Not clear yet". Kept shortlist items get no follow-up: a missing trigger is "Not clear yet". A workflow added at the list uses one of the two if any are left. Never ask about inputs, rules, steps, edge cases or size (Deconstruct settles the detail). A size the student volunteers goes under *Current pain point*; frequency is recorded only from what the student said or the evidence showed, otherwise "Not clear yet".

**The do-more-of question, once per run,** when the answers so far are all friction:

**Ask:** "If that took no time at all, what would you do with it, or do more of?" *(your words)*
- *Why:* some of the best opportunities are work you'd do more of, not just work you'd do faster.

A piece it names becomes a candidate only when it names work. A payoff that isn't work is recorded under *What changes for the business* on the cards of the friction pieces known when the question was asked (the pieces it frees time for), never on pieces found later, and never as their **Goal**.

**An answer that names no work:** the open question's *Not sure?* line is the follow-up (it isn't one of the two). When that *Not sure?* line was already shown in this run, the follow-up is instead N-A14's not-sure follow-up, with one concrete example drawn from their registry (one process, one kind of work, never a list): "For example, in [process], is there a [report / check / message] [your team rebuilds / you rebuild] by hand each time?" *(your words)* [process] is the process the offer would pick (*Enough to choose from*, below), so on the team lens it's always the team's own (*The team's own work*). If it still names none, go to the offer below.

## Enough to choose from (N-A17)

- **At 3 or more candidates** after the open question (with its playback and follow-ups): go to the list (`registering-candidates.md`).
- **Once there are 5,** never prompt for more: no do-more-of question, no offer. The open question is still asked once.
- **With fewer than 3,** make the offer:

  **Ask:** "Want to look at one process more closely? I'd pick [process], because it serves your goal: [goal], and nothing's been found there yet. Say 'go ahead', 'show my processes', or 'that's enough'." *(your words)*

  [process] is the first process, in registry order, that serves a goal, has no candidate and no Workflow node, and hasn't been walked in this run (every process in **Walking**); [goal] is its goal (the first in order when it serves several, by the goal map), as written, without its final full stop. With every goal-serving process holding a candidate or a workflow, or walked, pick the first with none of these. A process walked in this run is never offered again, even when its walk found nothing. With no goals: "Want to look at one process more closely? I'd pick [process], where the work you've described seems slowest and nothing's been found there yet. Say 'go ahead', 'show my processes', or 'that's enough'." *(your words)* (your best reading of what the student has said; ask nothing to choose it).

  **Right after a walk** (the second offer), its first sentence is "Want to look at another process?", never "Want to look at one process more closely?" again: "Want to look at another process? I'd pick [process], because it serves your goal: [goal], and nothing's been found there yet. Say 'go ahead', 'show my processes', or 'that's enough'." *(your words)* With no goals: "Want to look at another process? I'd pick [process], where the work you've described seems slowest and nothing's been found there yet. Say 'go ahead', 'show my processes', or 'that's enough'." *(your words)*

  With no registry, or a registry with no Process nodes: "Want to look at one area of your work more closely? Name it, or say 'that's enough'." *(your words)* (the same after a walk).

  **The team's own work.** On the team form of the organizational lens, [process], "show my processes" and the shortlist go by the work the team's own functions do, as the evidence shows it, not only by who owns the process. The team's own functions are those the founding log line names ("…; the team's own functions: [names]"); an older registry without that clause: infer them from the student's role and the function owners. A process counts as the team's when one of those functions owns it, or when the evidence (the team's own SOPs, process guides, notes or typed requests, or what the student said) shows the team doing work in it. Never offer or list a process another team owns where nothing shows the team doing its work (a group the team hands work to or receives it from). A kept item whose work the team does inside a process another function owns stays under that process until the list, where it gets one question (`registering-candidates.md`, *Team work in another team's process*).
- **"Show my processes"** lists the processes (on the team lens, only the team's own, as above), numbered straight through as numbered list lines ("6. [process]: [c] candidates, [w] workflows"), their numbers continuing across the labels, under a "**Line of business:** [name]" label per line, with a blank line after each label so the list shows as a list. The student names one. That reply is part of the offer's turn.
- **"Go ahead"** takes the process named; **"that's enough"** goes to the list, or to the zero close (SKILL.md, *The close*) with no candidates.

## The walk

For the chosen process, name it with its goal ("[Process] (goal: [goal])", the goal's final full stop dropped; no goal clause when it serves none), then ask the walk question once, anchored in its most recent real run, in the student's words with its real start and end (when the student has said nothing about its start and end, take them from the Process node's description). Ask it open. Say this:

**Ask:** "Take the last time [process] ran, from [its start] to [its end]. Where did the time go, or where did it get stuck?" *(your words)* (Individual lens, except a personal-life registry: "Take the last time you did your part of [process], from [its start] to [its end]. Where did the time go, or where did it get stuck?" *(your words)*)

When neither the student nor the Process node's description gives a start or an end, never invent them: on any lens, say its no-start-or-end form: "Take the last time [process] ran. Where did the time go, or where did it get stuck?" *(your words)*
- *Why:* the places where time or results are lost, or where doing more would pay off, are where a workflow is worth building.
- *Example:* a property manager might say: "Turning over a unit. I chase three contractors by phone, then re-enter the dates in two calendars."
- *Not sure?* Tell me the one part you'd hand to someone else tomorrow if you could.

Organizational lens: the process end to end; Individual: the student's part of it. Then the playback and any follow-ups left (above), then the do-more-of question if it hasn't been asked. No size. With no registry, or no Process nodes, the walk is the question bank's next unasked question for the lens (below), for the area named.

**After the first walk,** with still fewer than 3 candidates, make the offer once more, in its after-a-walk form ("Want to look at another process? …", above), never for a process already walked. When the walk found nothing (a thin walk, or nothing named), the offer's message starts with the bridge line: "I'll leave that one for now." *(your words)* **After the second walk,** go to the list, or to the zero close with none. The progress file records the offers made, and **Walking** keeps every process walked in this run.

## Thin answers

Inside the walk, when an answer is too vague to name the work or the problem, at most once per walk. When an answer names vague work and no problem, this comes first; N-A22 only once the work is named.
1. Play back what you have: "So far I have: [their words]."
2. Ask one narrower question anchored in a real instance: "Think of the last time you did this. What happened first?"
3. If it's still thin, move on: record nothing for it, and never invent an opportunity and record it as theirs.

## The question banks (no registry, or no Process nodes)

The role question comes first (`part-1-reading-and-the-lens.md`). The walk then takes the next unasked question from the bank for the lens. One question at a time: the banks are a scaffold for you, not a list to paste. Adapt to the answers, skip what the evidence or the open question already covered, and ask for concrete examples ("I spend 30 minutes every Monday formatting a status report from three Jira boards" is far more useful than "I do reporting").

**Individual Lens — Discovery Questions**

1. **Role & responsibilities** — What is your role? What are you accountable for?
2. **Repetitive tasks** — What tasks do you perform daily or weekly that feel repetitive, tedious, or low-value?
3. **Information synthesis** — Where do you spend time gathering, combining, or making sense of information from multiple sources?
4. **Multi-step processes** — What workflows involve multiple handoffs, approvals, or sequential steps?
5. **Quality & consistency** — Where do errors, inconsistencies, or quality issues tend to creep in?
6. **Communication overhead** — What recurring communications (status updates, reports, summaries) take more time than they should?
7. **Decision-making** — What decisions require you to weigh multiple factors or reference past precedents?

**Organizational Lens — Discovery Questions**

1. **Business objectives & outcomes** — What are your top 2-3 business objectives or OKRs? What outcomes drive success?
2. **Value chain & core processes** — What are the key end-to-end processes that deliver on those outcomes? (lead-to-close, hire-to-onboard, order-to-fulfill, etc.)
3. **Cross-functional handoffs** — Where do processes cross team boundaries? Where do things fall through cracks?
4. **Bottlenecks & cycle time** — Which processes take too long? Where do things stall waiting on people, approvals, or information?
5. **Consistency & quality risk** — Where do different people handle the same process differently? Where does inconsistency create risk?
6. **Visibility & measurement gaps** — Which processes lack metrics on performance, cycle time, or quality?
7. **Scale constraints** — Which processes break when volume increases? What works for 10 customers but not 100?
