# Personas

Four kinds of student run this interview. The pages written are identical for
all four — the same six types, the same frontmatter, the same `SCHEMA.md`.
What changes is the vocabulary and what each page stands for.

The persona is **never asked cold.** The first question (N-SR5) invites the
student to say who the registry is for, and you work it out from the answer
(below); the card shows it on its **For:** line, so the student can correct
it. It is **not stored** in any page: the founding `registry/log.md` entry
says in plain words who the registry is for, the Business page's identity
sentence usually implies it ("a household of four" vs. "a boutique
consultancy"), and `analyze` and `deconstruct` read the log line.

| Persona | Business page | Lines of Business | Functions (the owners) | Processes | Analyze lens default |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Founder or owner** | the company | distinct offerings, or one default line named after the company | working alone: three or four hats named for the area of work; with a team: the roles who do the work | the processes that deliver each line | Organizational; Individual offered second |
| **CEO or business leader** | the company, or the unit they lead | business units or offerings | the departments that report in; direct reports as `lead:` only on request | the enterprise processes that deliver each unit, at the level the leader actually sees | Organizational |
| **Professional inside an organization** | their team, department, or function | the services the team provides to the rest of the company, or one default line named after the team | the roles on the team the student named (or the team itself when none were named), plus the groups the team hands work to and receives it from | the processes the team runs or takes part in | Leading the team: Organizational, scoped to the team's processes. Working in it: Individual first (their part of the team's processes); Organizational for the team offered second |
| **Personal life** | the person or household, titled as they choose | the areas of life they want to run better: household, finances, health, family, learning, a side project | one per role that owns routines — "Me", "Partner", "Household admin" — never a person's name; at least one, since every routine needs an owner | the recurring routines that deliver each area: monthly budgeting, meal planning, travel planning, school logistics | Individual |

## Working out who it's for

Read the answer to N-SR5 (and any pasted source) for these signals:

| Signal | Persona |
|---|---|
| "my business", "my firm", "I run", "I founded", "my clients", customers, revenue, a company they own | Founder or owner |
| "the company I run", "my division", the whole company or a division with its own departments reporting in, a CEO, GM or president title | CEO or business leader |
| "my team", "my department", "I run the [X] team at [company]", "at [company] I", internal customers, "the rest of the company", a Director, Head of, VP or manager title over one team or function, a specialist title inside an organization | Professional inside an organization |
| household, family, kids, home, health, personal finances, "my life" | Personal life |

When one persona's signals are clear, use it. The more specific signal wins:
"I run the [X] team at [company]" is a team inside a larger organization, even
though "I run" on its own suggests a founder. When they conflict ("my team"
could be a company the student leads or a team inside one) or there are none,
ask the one follow-up in the interview guide. A side business the student
owns is the founder persona, even if they also mention their employer. A
team inside a larger company is the professional persona even when the
student runs it; whether they lead it or work in it goes in the founding log
line (below). They lead it when they say they run, lead, manage or head it,
or give a Director, Head of, VP or manager title for it.

## The founding log line

The founding `registry/log.md` entry says who the registry is for in exactly
one of these forms, never reworded, because `analyze` picks its lens from it
and `analyze` and `deconstruct` switch to personal-life wording from it:

| Who | The form |
|---|---|
| Founder or owner, working alone | set up for a business the owner runs alone |
| Founder or owner, with a team | set up for a business the owner runs with a team |
| CEO or business leader | set up for the company or unit the owner leads |
| Professional, leading the team | set up for the team the owner leads inside a larger organization; the team's own functions: [names] |
| Professional, working in the team | set up for the team the owner works in; the team's own functions: [names] |
| Personal life | set up for the owner's personal life |

For a team (the two professional forms), the line ends with one added clause, "; the team's own functions: [names]", naming the Functions for the roles on the team itself, never the groups it hands work to or receives it from. `analyze` offers only the processes those functions own on the team lens.

## Owners

The owner of each process is drafted in Phase 2 from what the student said
about who does the work. The functions are exactly the owners used.

- **Founder working alone:** three or four broad hats, named for the area of
  work: for example Sales, Delivery, Marketing, Operations. Use only the hats
  that own a process; never offer a longer starter set.
- **Founder with a team:** the roles the student named ("my two consultants"
  → Consulting; "an assistant who does the books" → Finance and Admin); where
  none fits a process, the area of work.
- **CEO or business leader:** the departments that report in, by the names
  the student uses.
- **Professional:** each role on the team the student named becomes the
  owner of the work it does ("two analysts" → Analytics; "a deal desk lead" →
  Deal Desk), or the team itself when no roles were named; plus the groups
  the team hands work to or receives it from (Finance, Legal, a partner team)
  for theirs. Never merge named roles into one owner for the whole team.
- **Personal life:** roles ("Me", "Partner", "Household admin"), never the
  person's name and never business functions. At least one.

A function is named for the area or role, never for the person; the person,
if the student wants them recorded, goes in `lead:`. A function's one-sentence
description says what it takes care of ("The household routines and the
kids' logistics.").

## Sources to paste

What N-SR5's "Or paste…" can take, beyond a website's About or Services text.
Mention these only when the student asks what would help:

- **Founder or owner:** a services page or offerings list, a team list, this year's plan or OKRs, a process inventory or SOP folder.
- **CEO or business leader:** the org chart, the annual plan or board deck, the business-unit list, any process inventory.
- **Professional inside an organization:** the team's charter or mission statement, your job description, the team's service catalogue or intake form, any runbooks.
- **Personal life:** nothing formal is expected — a list of the areas you want to run better is enough; a shared calendar, a budget spreadsheet, or a chores list helps if you have one.

## Nouns

The student hears the word in each cell, once the persona is known. Adjust
the words around a swapped noun so the sentence reads naturally ("your
household's goals", "what your household is for"); everything else stays
exact.

| Guide says | CEO or business leader | Professional | Personal life |
|---|---|---|---|
| business / your business | company / your company (or the unit you lead) | team / your team | household / your household (or "your life" for one person) |
| line of business / line | business unit / unit | service | area |
| process | process | process | routine |
| owner | owner | owner | role |

The founder or owner uses the guide's nouns as written. In N-SR7, the
personal-life form says: "A **routine** is recurring work that keeps one area of your life running, and its **role** is who takes care of it." in place of the process and owner definition.

## Personal life: the privacy line

Said once, before the card (so before anything is saved): "This registry will describe money, health, and family. Keep it on your computer or in a private repository."

## Sketches (shown, never copied)

**Professional:** Business "Data Platform Team" — *"The data platform team for a regional insurer: owns the warehouse, the pipelines, and the reporting layer."* One default line "Data Platform". Owners: Data Platform (the team), Finance, Claims, Underwriting. Processes: Pipeline Change Request (owner: data-platform), Monthly Close Reporting (owner: finance), Ad-hoc Analysis Request (owner: data-platform).

**Personal life:** Business "The Okafor Household" — *"A household of four; running well means the admin is invisible and weekends are free."* `## Objectives`: fewer evenings lost to admin; one weekly money check instead of surprises. Areas: Household, Finances, Kids. Roles: Me, Partner (both with `lead:` left blank). Routines: Meal Planning (owner: me), School Logistics (owner: me), Monthly Budget Review (owner: partner).
