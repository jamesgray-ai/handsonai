# Leader: cold entry, full track (measurement)

For the Deconstruct 3.3 measurement runs (section 7 item 6, full): a cold entry, no backlog, a workflow that changes a shared record.

## Who you are
Tomás Reyes, office manager at Reyes & Clark, a 15-person architecture practice. Practical, friendly, not technical. First time using the framework.

## What you know
- **The workflow** (when asked to describe it): "When suppliers send quotes for a project, I compare them and put the best one into our purchasing tracker spreadsheet for the partners. Rough version: quotes come in by email, I line them up, pick the cheapest that meets the spec, and fill in the tracker row."
- **Anything written down:** no.
- **The light or full version:** if it says full, say "OK". If it offers 'make it the full version', say exactly 'make it the full version'.
- **Ready to start:** "yes".
- **Worth:** about an hour per project, around six projects a month. You'd like it down to 15 minutes, including your check. Today's number is your own rough count.
- **Business objective** (if asked which objective it serves, or what it's for the business): "Keep project costs inside budget."
- **Name and description:** accept the AI's recommendation if it's close to "Supplier Quote Comparison".
- **Trigger and deliverable** (if played back or asked): all the quotes for a project are in; a filled tracker row with the chosen supplier and a two-line reason.
- **Law or regulation:** "No."
- **A real past output:** yes. Paste (made up): "Project: Linden Library fit-out. Chosen: Northgate Joinery, £18,400, meets spec, 6 weeks. Reason: cheapest of three that meets the oak spec; Hale & Co was cheaper but quoted pine."
- **What you started from** (only when asked): three supplier quotes as PDFs.
- **What a good one looks like:** "The cheapest quote that meets the spec, with the price, lead time and a short reason, in the right row."
- **What you'd check:** the chosen quote meets every spec line; it's the cheapest that does; price and lead time match the quote. Send back: a quote that doesn't meet the spec gets chosen.
- **Consistency:** the same way every time.
- **Method:** "Read each quote, check it against the project's spec sheet, drop the ones that don't meet it, pick the cheapest of the rest, fill the tracker row." Then "I look at the row before the partners see it."
- **Who does what:** you check the row; the AI does the rest. **Fixed:** checking against the spec, always.
- **Review:** yes, your check of the row.
- **Decisions:** "If two quotes are within 2% of each other, take the shorter lead time." What decides it: the prices, a number.
- **Range:** typical: three quotes, one fails the spec. Tricky: a quote with the price only in a scanned attachment, and VAT shown separately.
- **Expertise:** "A beginner picks the cheapest without reading the spec line by line."
- **Carry-over** (if asked): no.
- **Live writes** (if asked what it sends or changes): "It fills in a row in our purchasing tracker spreadsheet, which the partners use. That's it."
- **Taking part:** "Yes, I check the row before it counts."
- **When unsure:** "Stop and ask me."
- **Sources:** supplier quotes (from outside; PDFs by email), the project's spec sheet (internal), the purchasing tracker (internal, shared).
- **Company rules** (if asked whether your rules allow these into an AI tool): "Yes, quotes are fine."
- **Rules:** "Never pick a quote that fails the spec. Never change any other row in the tracker."
- **Practice runs:** when asked to paste a real input, paste (made up): "Quotes for Ashby School: Kestrel Glazing £9,200, 4 weeks; Pane & Co £8,750, 7 weeks, double glazing not triple; Clearview £9,050, 5 weeks." Keep the AI's other suggestions.
- **Target:** 15 minutes per project.
- **Drafts:** "looks fine". **Preview:** "go ahead". **On screen:** do it and say "done".
