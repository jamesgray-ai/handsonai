# Brief format

Every brief follows this shape, in this order. It is a **format only**: never copy names, companies, or details from any example into a new brief.

```
Brief — [Company], call [date].

Hypothesis: they took the call because [one reason, tied to a recent change].

Person: [Name], [role] since [when] ([source]); [one recent post or statement] ([source]).

Likely priorities: [two or three priorities for their team].

Sources: [every source used, e.g. LinkedIn, press release [date], our CRM notes].
```

Notes on the shape:

- **Lead line.** By default the brief leads with the Hypothesis. If the CRM shows past work with this company, a `Past work:` line comes first, then the Hypothesis (rule D1).
- **One hypothesis**, one sentence, tied to something that changed recently.
- **Person** names the role and one thing they said or posted recently, each with its source.
- **Likely priorities**: two or three, short.
- **Sources** lists every source the brief drew on. Every fact above also names its source inline.
- **Questions**: end with a `Questions to consider:` line holding one or two questions Dana might not think of. Dana adds her own at the review pause.
- **Not found** goes in plainly, e.g. `Person: Sam Ortiz, founder (calendar invite); no LinkedIn profile or recent posts found.`
- **Flags** for anything uncertain: mark the item `(unconfirmed)` and say why in one line under the brief.
- Keep the whole brief to one page.
